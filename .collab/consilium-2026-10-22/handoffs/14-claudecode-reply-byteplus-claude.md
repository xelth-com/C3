## Framing first

The proposal answers a diagnosis the record does not support. Pass 1 raised 8; a worker fixed all 8; pass 2 found 4, two of them "the remaining case" of pass-1 blockers. That pattern is not *the finder's frame was confirmed by its own remedy*. It is *the remedy was a direction and the invariant was never written down*. Three labs proposing three designs fixes the **choice** problem; it does not produce the artifact that closes the loop — a test that fails before the fix and passes after. Without that, pass 3 will again say "the fix closed the simple case".

So: yes to solvers proposing remedies, but as the **second** intervention. Cheaper first moves the brief does not list:

- **Blank-slate re-design by the finder, without its own remedy text.** Frame-confirmation comes mostly from re-reading its own sentence; deleting it costs nothing.
- **Second-lab review of the diff, not design before it.** Reviewing 50 changed lines beats designing from a pack, and targets the missed case directly.
- **A deterministic schedule/state harness** for the admission family (F91-1/2/3 are all read-state-then-act races). One fixture harness kills the family; no amount of prose design does.
- **A no-panel fast path** where the design space is singular (F91-3 is an ordering fix).
- **An explicit "no proposal adopted" outcome.** Without it a panel always yields a fix and launders the decision.

Missing from the sketch: the **cost model**. Window %, not wall-clock, is scarce — N parallel solvers cost N × 280 s of window for one pass's wall-clock, and blockers arrive in families. Budget per family, cap at 2 solvers, pre-commit the window. Also, **vendor diversity is not independence**: shared training data and a shared brief give correlated proposals, and `--exclude-vendor` can leave one eligible solver. Require ≥2, else skip and record why. What I would need to choose: a replay of the F91-1 brief to two labs, diffed, to see whether proposals actually diverge (RC5).

## Q1

Separation is sound; the roles are mis-assigned. **Verifier ≠ finder.** The finder's acceptance criteria are already on record (`trigger`, `verification`), so make it a *challenger* with the mandate to find what the test misses, not to bless the fix. The verifier of record should be **the solver's test, rejected if it passes before the fix** — a third member only for judgement calls (severity, design), never for mechanical invariants. Failure modes: a pack-fed solver proposes plausible-but-wrong fixes the coordinator cannot rank without reading the code itself; the standing finder holds an effective veto over foreign fixes, so "the coordinator picks" collapses to "the finder approves" unless the finder's criteria are frozen *before* proposals land; and per-blocker panels multiply cost across a family. See finding #9.
